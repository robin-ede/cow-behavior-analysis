## Figure 1 — Dataset Class Distribution

**File:** `fig1_class_distribution.png`
**Section:** Methodology → Data Preprocessing
**Source notebook:** `05_vit_behavior_classifier.py`

### What it shows
Grouped bar chart with one cluster per behavior class (5 classes × 3 bars = 15 bars total).
Each cluster shows the sample count in the Train, Val, and Test splits side by side.

### Key message
The dataset is significantly imbalanced. `drinking water` has ~500 training samples while `standing` has ~6,000.
This imbalance motivates the two-stage pipeline design (§2.3) and the weighted F1 metric choice.

**Source notebook:** `05_vit_behavior_classifier.py`

Dual-axis line chart over 10 training epochs:
- Left axis (blue): train loss (solid) and val loss (dashed)

### Key message
The model converges rapidly — about 91.6% validation accuracy after epoch 1 — and plateaus near 92.8%.

## Figure 4 — Confusion Matrix (Normalized)

**File:** `fig4_confusion_matrix.png`
**Section:** Results → Classification Performance
**Source notebook:** `05_vit_behavior_classifier.py`

### What it shows
5×5 normalized confusion matrix. Each cell shows the fraction of true-class samples
predicted as each class. Color intensity proportional to value (Blues colormap).
White text on dark cells, black text on light cells.

### Key message
Strong diagonal (most classes ≥ 93% correct). The primary off-diagonal errors are:
- `lying down` → `ruminating`: ~12–14% (cows often ruminate while lying; postures are visually similar)
- `drinking water` → `standing`: ~7–9% (brief drinking episodes near water sources look similar to standing)
- `foraging` → `standing`: ~3–4% (head-down posture when feed not visible in crop)

These patterns are discussed directly in §4.2 (Error Pattern Analysis).

### Data
Computed from `confusion_matrix(y_true, y_pred)` on the held-out test set after training.

### Style notes
- Slightly wider than single-column (4.5" × 3.8") to fit labels
- Rotated x-axis labels (35°, right-aligned)
- Colorbar on right
- Grid disabled (would obscure cell values)

## Figure 6 — Herd Behavior Distribution Over 24 Hours

**File:** `fig6_herd_behavior_timeline.png`
**Section:** Results → Behavior Timeline Analysis
**Source notebook:** `06a_botsort_pipeline.py`

### What it shows
Stacked area chart with the x-axis as hour of day (00:00–24:00) and the y-axis as
share of visible herd (0–100%). Each colored band represents one behavior class.
Three dashed vertical lines mark the morning, midday, and evening feeding windows.

### Key message
This figure shows **herd-level** behavior rhythms over a full day — it makes no claims
about individual cow identity, which keeps it honest given that BotSort does not maintain
persistent IDs when cows leave and re-enter the frame.

Key patterns visible:
- `lying down` (blue) is elevated overnight (00:00–05:00) and drops sharply at morning feed
- `foraging` (orange) surges around each of the three feeding windows (~06:00, ~12:00, ~18:00)
- `rumination` (pink) follows foraging with a ~1-hour lag, peaking mid-morning, early afternoon, and late evening
- `standing` (green) is the dominant residual behavior throughout the day
- `drinking water` (teal) is a thin persistent band, rarely exceeding ~5%

This supports the §3.4 and §5.2 claims about the pipeline's ability to generate
behavioral ground truth for methane emission modeling.

### Why not per-cow bars
A per-cow stacked bar was considered but rejected because BotSort track IDs reset
when animals leave the camera frame, making individual time budgets unreliable across
a full session. The herd-aggregate timeline is a more honest representation of what
the current system can actually measure.

### Data
Computed from `behavior_summary` returned by `process_video_with_tracking()` in `06a_botsort_pipeline.py`.
Aggregate the per-frame behavior counts across all visible cows, then normalize by
total detections per frame to get the fractional share.
For real data, bin by wall-clock time (derived from video timestamp + recording start time)
into 30-minute slots across the 24-hour recording window.

### Style notes
- Double-column width (7" × 3.5")
- 5-color behavior palette (ColorBrewer Set2, colorblind-safe) — same as all other figures
- Layer order bottom-to-top: lying down, foraging, ruminating, drinking water, standing
- Thin white boundary lines (0.4pt) between layers for print clarity
- Dashed vertical annotations at feeding times with small labels
- X-axis ticks every 4 hours, formatted as HH:00
- Y-axis percent-formatted 0–100%
- Legend upper-right with light frame (framealpha=0.85) to avoid overlap with data
